👋 Hi, I’m Nathanael Paskal

Welcome to my GitHub academic space! This repository of projects, notes, and experimental explorations reflects my ongoing journey in computer engineering education, computer architecture, and system-level design. I am currently an Informatics and Computer Engineering Education student at the State University of Jakarta, where I engage deeply with teaching, research, and hands-on technical development, while continuously refining my understanding of how computing systems operate from the lowest level of hardware all the way up to modern software abstractions.

My academic interests revolve around instruction set architectures, processor design, digital systems, compiler behavior, and the practical relationship between hardware execution and software semantics—areas that constantly challenge me to think critically about performance, correctness, and design trade-offs. I enjoy transforming theoretical concepts into working prototypes, and I often find myself experimenting with HDL designs, simulation frameworks, and co-verification tools (it’s fun, I promise xD).

🔧 Personal Architecture-Related Projects

Although still evolving and intentionally kept at an exploratory scale, some of my personal projects include:

A custom RISC-V RV32I processor designed in SystemVerilog, featuring basic pipeline considerations and verified using Python + cocotb testbenches.

A simple educational MIPS-style processor, built to solidify my understanding of datapaths, FSM control, and the relationship between ISA operations and hardware sequencing.

Both projects are part of my effort to integrate teaching, learning, and practical experimentation—serving as platforms for further deep dives into CPU design, verification strategies, and ISA behavior.

🌱 What You’ll Find Here

You’ll find a mixture of:

academic exercises and research notes

teaching materials from courses I assist with

HDL experiments (working and not-so-perfect ones 😄)

small utilities and prototypes from my learning process

documentation of challenges, insights, and future improvements

I aim to maintain this space as both a personal archive and an open learning resource—something transparent, continuously updated, and hopefully useful to anyone exploring similar topics.
