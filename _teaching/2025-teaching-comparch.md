As part of my role as an Informatics, Coding, and Artificial Intelligence instructor at **SMKN 31 Jakarta**, I had the unique opportunity (and mild existential crisis xD) of teaching **freshman high school students** topics that—if we're being honest—were never meant to be encountered by humans who just learned what a _computer_ is.

Despite this, the classes became an unexpectedly exciting adventure in educational chaos, structured learning, and the occasional "Sir… why does the CPU have _stages_?" moments.

## Course Overview

I developed and delivered a rather ambitious curriculum, which in hindsight looks like a blend of modern computer architecture fundamentals, programming theory, and the kind of deep-dive knowledge you typically reserve for second-year university students.  
But hey, curiosity is curiosity—right? 😅

## What I Taught (a.k.a. “Why did I think 15-year-olds needed this?”)

- **CPU architecture basics** — components, datapaths, memory hierarchy, and the magical world of instruction execution.
- **Programming language hierarchy** — from machine code → assembly → high-level languages, featuring the classic “This is why Python feels magical” explanation.
- **How programs actually run** — compilers, interpreters, and the hidden machinery that turns a file into something the computer obeys.
- **Fundamental algorithms** — logic, problem-solving, and why you shouldn’t fear loops (even though they sometimes feel like infinite ones IRL xD).

## Educational Philosophy

While the material occasionally felt like a “WTF did I just put in this curriculum?” moment, the goal was always to empower students to think _deeply_ about computing—from conceptual understanding to hands-on experimentation.  
And surprisingly (or miraculously), many of them thrived. Turns out, high schoolers can handle more than expected when you give them the tools and the hype.

## Reflections

Teaching this class taught **me** as much as it taught them:  
that enthusiasm beats complexity,  
that abstraction is your best friend,  
and that watching a 15-year-old explain a CPU pipeline using hand gestures is one of the purest joys of the profession 😂.
